VT340 Phase 4 controlled report-delay experiment
================================================

Purpose
-------

Test whether CPU1's four-byte LK201 command interval after response 5 is the timing
boundary that prevents the native sentinel/checkpoint order. This is a
debugger-only dependency experiment, not proposed hardware behavior.

Method
------

`phase4-report-delay-experiment.lua` activates only after CPU1 accepts its
fifth regular DAT9 response at 3BC8.  For the immediately following four-byte
report, it changes R0 to 1 at both DJNZ loops (3B6E and 3B70).  Calls, stack
operations, all three outer iterations, the SBUF writes, and both processors'
program counters remain native.  The intervention removes itself at 3AE1.

The normal firmware routine is:

    3B64  CLR TI / MOV SBUF,A
    3B68  MOV A,#03
    3B6A  PUSH R0
    3B6C  MOV R0,#00
    3B6E  DJNZ R0,3B6E
    3B70  DJNZ R0,3B70
    3B72  DEC A / JNZ 3B6C

CPU1 configures TMOD=21, TH1=F3, PCON=FF at 3B78.  With the schematic 12 MHz
8031 clock and SMOD=1, the internal UART rate is approximately 4807.7 baud;
one 10-bit 8N1 character is approximately 2.08 ms.  The two 256-iteration
DJNZ loops repeated three times take approximately 3.072 ms plus surrounding
instructions.  The delay is therefore deliberate UART pacing and must not be
removed in the production driver.

Observed emulation-time path
----------------------------

    8.222098  CPU1 accepts response 5
    8.222281  shortened four-byte keyboard command block completes
    8.222291  CPU1 emits pulse 6
    8.222432  CPU1 accepts response 6 from a native CPU2 table transition
    8.222455  CPU1 writes the authentic pending sentinel 7FF2=10
    8.228779  CPU2 executes its authentic 136D write; 7FF2 becomes 00
    8.236053  CPU1 accepts the authentic zero
   13.194727  CPU2 writes the authentic result 7FF2=80
   13.194734  CPU1 observes result 80 at 3A3A

Result
------

Shortening only this one report interval is sufficient for unmodified ROM
control flow to obtain sentinel 10 -> checkpoint zero -> result 80 ordering. The experiment proves
that the report/table timing relationship is the immediate dependency.  It
does not justify changing the 8031 clock, instruction timing, UART delay, or
mailbox semantics.  A valid correction must explain why real matched firmware
places CPU1 pulse 6 within CPU2's pre-136D table interval.
